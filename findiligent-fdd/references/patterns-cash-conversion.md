# FinDiligent Pattern Library — Cash Conversion

Version 1.0 · 1 Nov 2026 · Author: CA Ayush Bansal

Eleven patterns on the book's central question: whether reported earnings turn into cash. They use the same nine fields as the Revenue Quality library (see its "How to read an entry") and the vocabulary of the four definition docs. Every hypothesis and effect is indicative, not foolproof.

## CC01 — Receivables growing faster than earnings

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | EBITDA up while trade receivables or DSO rise faster; cash conversion (R2) falling |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Deliberate credit extension to win customers, with sound collection. (b) Revenue recognised ahead of cash: "paper EBITDA" (book: EBITDA +20%, receivables +50%, p.13). (c) Collection problems with specific customers or payors. (d) Related-party sales collected slowly |
| Financial effects | Earnings overstated relative to cash; working capital absorbs funding; possible under-provision for doubtful debts (earnings layer 2) |
| Possible outcomes | Write-offs; higher debt to fund working capital; valuation pressure |
| Evidence — public | DSO trend on the W2 basis; receivable ageing and loss-allowance note; related-party receivables; operating cash flow vs EBITDA |
| Evidence — diligence | Customer-wise ageing; subsequent collections; credit notes and write-offs after year-end; invoice-by-invoice receipt allocation (p.66) |
| Credit reading | Working-capital funding need rises with growth; receivable collateral may be weaker than it looks |
| Equity reading | Value on cash conversion, not EBITDA growth |
| Source | [Book] pp.13, 22, 65–66 |

## CC02 — Unbilled revenue and contract assets building up

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Unbilled revenue or contract assets growing faster than revenue; large unbilled balances at year-end |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Milestone billing in long contracts, with billing following soon after. (b) Optimistic estimates of progress or package value (book: in-patient packages accrued at year-end, p.54; percentage-of-completion revenue, p.66). (c) Disputed work that will not be billed in full |
| Financial effects | Revenue and EBITDA potentially overstated (earnings layer 2); receivables include judgemental balances |
| Possible outcomes | Revenue reversals when the final bill is lower; delayed cash |
| Evidence — public | Contract assets note; revenue-recognition policy; ratio of unbilled to revenue over time |
| Evidence — diligence | Trace year-end unbilled amounts to bills raised in the following one to two months (p.54) |
| Credit reading | Exclude unbilled balances from receivable-based borrowing bases until billed |
| Equity reading | Adjust earnings for any shortfall between accrued and billed value |
| Source | [Book] pp.54, 60, 65–66 |

## CC03 — Inventory build-up

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Inventory or DIO rising faster than revenue; inventory write-downs recurring |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Planned stock-up for expected demand or supply security. (b) Weak demand or obsolescence (book: DIO doubling from 45 to 90 days, p.64). (c) Slow-moving or accumulating stock not provided for (p.63). (d) Overheads parked in inventory, inflating EBITDA (p.64) |
| Financial effects | Cash tied up; EBITDA overstated by under-provision or over-absorbed overheads (earnings layer 2) |
| Possible outcomes | Write-offs; discounting to clear stock; margin hit |
| Evidence — public | DIO trend (W2); inventory note by category; write-down history; capacity utilisation |
| Evidence — diligence | SKU-wise quantity reconciliation and consumption rate (p.63); ageing from purchase date; overhead absorption method |
| Credit reading | Inventory collateral may need a deeper haircut |
| Equity reading | Normalise earnings for write-downs that recur |
| Source | [Book] pp.22, 60, 63–64 |

## CC04 — Payables stretch

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Trade payables or DPO rising faster than revenue; payables ageing beyond normal terms; supplier-finance arrangements disclosed |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Better terms negotiated through scale. (b) Liquidity stress: suppliers paid late to conserve cash (p.67). (c) Reverse factoring or channel financing sitting inside payables (pp.67–68). (d) Pre-transaction cash conservation |
| Financial effects | Operating cash flow flattered; the excess over normal terms and any supplier finance are debt-like (V3), so net debt is understated |
| Possible outcomes | Supplier strain, supply interruption, price increases; a working-capital unwind that consumes cash |
| Evidence — public | DPO trend (W2); Ind AS 7 supplier-finance disclosures (annual periods from 1 April 2025); MSME payables overdue disclosure; operating vs financing cash flow |
| Evidence — diligence | Ageing against contracted terms; supplier statements; financing agreements |
| Credit reading | Count supplier finance and stretched payables as debt in leverage tests |
| Equity reading | Normalise the peg for the stretch; otherwise the buyer funds the unwind |
| Source | [Book] pp.22, 67–68, 80 |

## CC05 — Working-capital starvation before a sale

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | In the year before a sale, refinancing or fundraise: inventory and receivables fall sharply, collections accelerate, cash rises |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Genuine working-capital efficiency programme. (b) Deliberate starvation to boost cash and equity proceeds at exit (p.72). (c) Seasonal low point at the balance-sheet date |
| Financial effects | Net debt falls and equity proceeds rise while enterprise value is unchanged; the business may be handed over below its normal working capital |
| Possible outcomes | Post-deal cash injection to restore inventory and credit terms; strained customer relationships |
| Evidence — public | September vs March readings (W3); days against the three-year normal level; deal or fundraising announcements |
| Evidence — diligence | Monthly balance sheets; purchase and collection patterns in the final months |
| Credit reading | Look through the year-end net-debt figure to the normal working-capital need |
| Equity reading | Set the peg on the normal level, not the starved closing position |
| Source | [Book] pp.12, 72, 82–83 |

## CC06 — Operating costs capitalised

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | EBITDA margin up while capex, intangible assets or CWIP rise; R&M falling; ROCE falling; capitalisation threshold lowered |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Genuine investment programme with future benefit. (b) Routine repairs, maintenance contracts or consumables capitalised (book: INR 50 lakh AMC/CMC, p.7; low-value additions, p.62). (c) Development or software costs capitalised aggressively (p.60) |
| Financial effects | EBITDA overstated (earnings layer 2); capital employed inflated, so ROCE falls even as EBITDA rises (p.62) |
| Possible outcomes | Restatement; later impairment; EBITDA lower once costs are expensed |
| Evidence — public | Capex intensity (R3); intangible additions with little amortisation; R&M intensity; capitalisation policy and any change in it |
| Evidence — diligence | Fixed asset register additions by value and nature; AMC and CMC contract terms |
| Credit reading | Base leverage on EBITDA after reversing doubtful capitalisation |
| Equity reading | Reverse capitalised opex before applying a multiple |
| Source | [Book] pp.6–7, 38, 60, 62 |

## CC07 — Deferred maintenance

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Low or falling R&M intensity; low net/gross block on key assets; little recent capex; warranty expiries ahead; rising power use per unit |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) New assets that genuinely need little maintenance yet. (b) Maintenance deferred to flatter EBITDA and cash (book: maintenance need of \~INR 200 m deferred, p.13; medical equipment at 29% net/gross with low R&M, p.61). (c) Repairs capitalised instead (CC06) |
| Financial effects | EBITDA and cash flow overstated; a catch-up backlog that is debt-like (V3); a recurring run-rate cost in earnings layer 4 or 5 |
| Possible outcomes | Breakdowns, downtime, catch-up capex after the deal |
| Evidence — public | R&M intensity excluding land (R3); asset vintage; maintenance capex vs depreciation where disclosed; warranty or AMC disclosures; kWh per unit trend (p.40) |
| Evidence — diligence | Asset condition, maintenance budgets vs actuals, warranty schedules (p.41) |
| Credit reading | Backlog and maintenance capex compete with debt service for cash, even though the bank-convention DSCR does not deduct them; read DSCR alongside asset health |
| Equity reading | Deduct the backlog from equity value; reflect the run-rate cost in maintainable earnings |
| Source | [Book] pp.13, 40–41, 61–62, 79 |

## CC08 — Revenue-to-cash gap

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Cash collections well below revenue after allowing for receivable and advance movements; cash-heavy collection businesses; collections said to be spent directly on construction or capex |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Timing items: TDS withheld, gateway fees, contra settlements, write-offs (N1). (b) Collections outside the banking system (book: education revenue 669 vs bank receipts 286, p.52). (c) Fictitious or inflated revenue |
| Financial effects | Revenue quality in doubt; if (b) or (c), revenue and EBITDA overstated and cash leaked |
| Possible outcomes | Governance finding; restatement; deal abort |
| Evidence — public | Operating cash flow vs EBITDA; receivables vs revenue; auditor remarks; cash-in-hand balances; related-party flows |
| Evidence — diligence | Full revenue-to-cash reconciliation (p.48), with reconciling items by cause |
| Credit reading | Do not rely on reported earnings for repayment until the gap is explained |
| Equity reading | A governance red flag, not just an adjustment |
| Source | [Book] pp.48, 52 |

## CC09 — Trapped or unavailable cash

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Large cash or deposit balances alongside significant borrowings; lien-marked deposits; advances to promoters or group companies; cash in overseas subsidiaries |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Planned liquidity buffer or cash earmarked for capex. (b) Cash restricted by liens, margin money or reserves (p.71). (c) Cash moved to related parties (p.71). (d) Cash that cannot be upstreamed because of local rules (p.71) |
| Financial effects | Net debt understated if restricted or trapped cash is netted (V2); interest paid on debt while cash earns less |
| Possible outcomes | Liquidity shortfall despite reported cash; related-party recovery risk |
| Evidence — public | Cash and bank notes (lien-marked balances); loans and advances to related parties; interest income vs finance cost; segment cash by geography |
| Evidence — diligence | Bank confirmations; lien documents; repatriation constraints |
| Credit reading | Net only free cash; highlight related-party advances separately, outside net debt |
| Equity reading | Value only cash a buyer can actually use |
| Source | [Book] pp.22, 71, 79 |

## CC10 — Reliance on customer float

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Structurally negative working capital: customers pay in advance (tickets, fees, subscriptions, packages) and suppliers are paid later; cash balances swing with bookings |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) A durable business-model advantage. (b) A temporary float spike at the balance-sheet date (book: collections before a major event, p.16). (c) Float used to fund capex or losses, so cash is not freely available (p.51) |
| Financial effects | Cash and NWC look strong at the peak; part of the cash belongs to future obligations (contract liabilities, payables to partners); D19 debate over whether advances are NWC or debt-like |
| Possible outcomes | Cash drains if bookings slow; liquidity squeeze when growth stops |
| Evidence — public | Contract liabilities and customer advances; September vs March readings (W3); operating cash flow in slow periods |
| Evidence — diligence | Monthly float pattern; settlement terms with partners; trailing-average NWC |
| Credit reading | Do not count float as free liquidity; stress a bookings slowdown |
| Equity reading | Set the peg on a trailing average so the buyer does not pay for a temporary float peak (p.16) |
| Source | [Book] pp.16, 51, 83 |

## CC11 — Capex without returns

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Several years of growth capex while revenue or EBITDA stays flat; ROCE or gross cash return falling; large CWIP ageing |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Long gestation, with returns still to come. (b) Failed or under-utilised projects (p.62). (c) Capex inflated by capitalised opex (CC06) or by cash leakage on construction (p.52) |
| Financial effects | Capital employed grows without matching earnings; borrowings rise to fund it; impairment risk |
| Possible outcomes | Write-downs; leverage climbs; equity value lower than book value |
| Evidence — public | Capex intensity and asset turnover (R1, R3); ROCE trend; CWIP ageing schedule; utilisation commentary; debt growth vs EBITDA growth |
| Evidence — diligence | Project-level returns; capacity utilisation; budget vs actual capex |
| Credit reading | New debt for capex should be tested against the project's own cash generation |
| Equity reading | Rising EBITDA with falling ROCE is an early warning (p.62) |
| Source | [Book] pp.52, 62 |
