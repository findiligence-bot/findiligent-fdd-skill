# FinDiligent Pattern Library — Cost and Margin

Version 1.0 · 1 Nov 2026 · Author: CA Ayush Bansal

Twelve patterns on whether the reported cost base is complete, recurring and sustainable. They use the same nine fields as the Revenue Quality library and the vocabulary of the four definition docs. Every hypothesis and effect is indicative, not foolproof.

## CM01 — Input cost spike

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Material cost as % of revenue jumps; material or sector gross margin (E2) falls sharply |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Temporary commodity, freight or supply-chain shock, including war (p.20). (b) Structural loss of pricing power: costs cannot be passed on. (c) Mix shift to lower-margin products (RQ03). (d) Procurement inefficiency or leakage |
| Financial effects | Margin compression that dominates the margin walk (book: materials −571 bps of a −374 bps EBITDA margin fall, p.19; E4) |
| Possible outcomes | Recovery if temporary and passed through; lasting margin reset if structural |
| Evidence — public | Margin walk by cost line (E4); commodity price trends; management commentary on price increases and pass-through; peer margins |
| Evidence — diligence | Supplier-wise purchase prices; contracts with escalation clauses; product-level margin |
| Credit reading | Stress test on the lower margin until pass-through is evidenced |
| Equity reading | Do not normalise to the old margin without proof of pass-through |
| Source | [Book] pp.19–20 |

## CM02 — Employee cost falling as a share of revenue

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Employee cost % of revenue falls (book: 29.1% to 24.2%, p.19), or headcount grows while cost per head falls |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Genuine operating leverage from revenue growth on a stable team (p.28). (b) Senior roles left vacant (p.30). (c) Experienced staff replaced with cheaper juniors (book: R&D average cost −15%, p.29). (d) Work shifted to contractors or consultants (p.32) |
| Financial effects | EBITDA flattered by vacancies (a run-rate cost belongs in earnings layer 4 or 5); capability loss not visible in numbers |
| Possible outcomes | Cost rebounds when roles are filled; weaker product, delivery or innovation |
| Evidence — public | Headcount and cost per head (FTE where available, p.32); key managerial personnel changes; contract labour and professional fees trend |
| Evidence — diligence | Department-wise headcount and average cost (p.29); vacancy list; contractor agreements |
| Credit reading | Add back the vacancy cost before relying on the margin |
| Equity reading | Accept operating leverage only where headcount and quality are stable |
| Source | [Book] pp.19–20, 28–32 |

## CM03 — Pockets of rising pay

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Overall employee cost stable, but one function's cost or pay per head rising far faster than revenue (book: administration average cost +25%, p.29; legal department cost +40% against 15% revenue growth) |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Specialist hiring for growth. (b) Pay rises to promoter-related staff at above-market levels (p.29). (c) An inefficiency trap: rising cost, flat output (p.29) |
| Financial effects | Non-arm's-length pay is a normalisation in earnings layer 3; inefficiency is a sustainable cost that depresses margin |
| Possible outcomes | Governance concern; persistent overhead drag |
| Evidence — public | Related-party remuneration disclosures; managerial remuneration notes; function-level cost where disclosed |
| Evidence — diligence | Department-wise pay analysis; relationships of highly paid staff to promoters; productivity metrics per department |
| Credit reading | Treat excess related-party pay as leakage that could be stopped by covenant |
| Equity reading | Add back only the excess over market pay, with benchmark evidence |
| Source | [Book] p.29 |

## CM04 — Statutory compliance gaps

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Large labour-intensive workforce; heavy use of contractors or contract labour; low employee cost per head against peers; news of labour-law notices; the Labour Codes in force from 21 November 2025 widening the wage base |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Fully compliant, with costs already accrued. (b) Under-paid PF, ESI, bonus, overtime or minimum wages (book: overtime paid on basic instead of gross for \~5,000 workers, p.75). (c) Consultants who are employees in substance (sham contracts, p.32). (d) Contractor agencies not compliant, leaving the principal employer liable (p.33) |
| Financial effects | Recurring cost understated (earnings layer 2); accumulated arrears are debt-like (V3); the wider wage definition under the Codes may raise ongoing cost |
| Possible outcomes | Demands with interest and penalties; indemnity claims; deal delay or abort |
| Evidence — public | Employee cost per head vs peers; contingent liabilities for labour matters; contract labour disclosures; auditor remarks on statutory dues |
| Evidence — diligence | Employee-wise recomputation under the regime in force for each period (dated parameters); PF challans of contractors; consultant agreements |
| Credit reading | Arrears rank as near-term cash claims; the run-rate cost reduces DSCR |
| Equity reading | Seek specific indemnities in addition to price adjustment |
| Source | [Book] pp.32–34, 75, 79; Phase 2 R1–R4 |

## CM05 — High attrition behind strong margins

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | High or rising attrition in delivery or billable staff, alongside stable or improving margins |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Industry-normal churn at a cost already in the run-rate. (b) Instability masked by margins (book: \~40% attrition in billable IT staff, p.31). (c) Pay below market, creating future increment pressure |
| Financial effects | Recruitment, agency and retention costs may not be in the run-rate (earnings layer 4 or 5); delivery quality and revenue at risk |
| Possible outcomes | Wage inflation to retain staff; client attrition; margin squeeze |
| Evidence — public | Attrition disclosures (state the formula; the book uses leavers ÷ (opening + joiners), p.31); headcount trend; recruitment costs |
| Evidence — diligence | Monthly headcount movements; exit reasons; retention bonus commitments |
| Credit reading | Treat as an operating-risk flag on the durability of cash flow |
| Equity reading | Add sustainable retention costs before applying a multiple |
| Source | [Book] pp.29, 31 |

## CM06 — Pending wage settlement

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | A union agreement expired or under negotiation; news of wage demands or industrial action |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Settlement within the existing provision. (b) A settlement above the run-rate, applied retrospectively (book: union seeks 25%, management expects 15%, from the expiry date, p.30) |
| Financial effects | Run-rate employee cost rises (earnings layer 5, prospective pro-forma); retrospective arrears are debt-like (V3) |
| Possible outcomes | Margin reset; strike risk; disruption |
| Evidence — public | Contingent liabilities and commitments; management commentary on negotiations; news |
| Evidence — diligence | Agreement terms, settlement history, current offer and demand |
| Credit reading | Model the higher wage bill and the arrears outflow |
| Equity reading | Price both the annual cost and the one-time arrears |
| Source | [Book] p.30 |

## CM07 — Key-professional cost escalation

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Doctor, faculty or specialist compensation growing faster than the revenue they generate; renegotiated revenue-share terms; one-time payments to professionals |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Market-rate adjustment to retain talent. (b) Elevated revenue share agreed during stress now squeezing margin (book: 30–40% of centre revenue, p.44). (c) Specialty revenue falling while the professional's pay stays flat (p.44) |
| Financial effects | Sector gross margin (E2) compresses; a one-time buy-down payment may be a normalisation (View II) or a prepayment spread over the term (View I) — a judgement, not a rule (p.44) |
| Possible outcomes | Margin erosion by specialty; renewal risk when contracts expire within 1–3 years (p.45) |
| Evidence — public | Professional fees trend; related-party payments to doctor-promoters; specialty revenue disclosures |
| Evidence — diligence | Agreements: compensation model, lock-in, renewal dates; recomputation of variable pay against specialty revenue |
| Credit reading | Model renewal at market terms, not the current contract |
| Equity reading | Benchmark revised pay to market before accepting any add-back of the buy-down |
| Source | [Book] pp.44–45 |

## CM08 — Legal and professional cost spike

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Legal and professional cost up sharply (book: 22 to 35, with 10 relating to a High Court matter, pp.24, 35); new retainers; news of disputes or investigations |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) One-off transaction or advisory fees. (b) Litigation that implies an unprovided liability. (c) Retainers to related parties used to extract cash (p.36). (d) Regulatory investigation (p.39) |
| Financial effects | One-off fees are a normalisation (earnings layer 3); the underlying claim may be debt-like or contingent (V3); related-party retainers are leakage |
| Possible outcomes | Settlement outflows; governance findings; further legal cost until resolution |
| Evidence — public | Contingent liabilities note; litigation disclosures; related-party payments; auditor remarks; news |
| Evidence — diligence | Party-wise and nature-wise ledger; ledger narration scan for litigation, settlement, investigation (p.36); litigation schedule from legal diligence |
| Credit reading | Focus on the claim behind the cost, not the cost itself |
| Equity reading | Add back only fees tied to an event that has ended |
| Source | [Book] pp.24, 35–36, 39 |

## CM09 — Recurring "one-off" items

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Exceptional or one-off items in two of three years; management adjusted EBITDA consistently above reported; repeated restructuring or transformation charges |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Genuinely unrelated events that happened to recur. (b) Normal operating costs labelled exceptional (book: fines in FY24 and FY26, inventory write-downs every year, p.37). (c) Add-backs to lift value (book: INR 50 m failed integration added back, p.84) |
| Financial effects | Adjusted EBITDA overstated; each accepted add-back multiplies into value |
| Possible outcomes | Buyers and lenders reject the add-backs; valuation falls |
| Evidence — public | Exceptional items notes over three years; management adjustment reconciliations (layer 1b); the recurrence check in E3 and V8 |
| Evidence — diligence | Board minutes; nature of each item; whether costs continue after the period |
| Credit reading | Base covenants on EBITDA before recurring "one-offs" |
| Equity reading | "EBITDA is an opinion; cash is a fact" (p.84): the cash has left whatever the label |
| Source | [Book] pp.37, 74–75, 84 |

## CM10 — Promoter and related-party costs

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Promoter-owned premises with nil or below-market rent; founder working unpaid or underpaid; owner management fees; personal or lifestyle expenses in company accounts |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Arm's-length arrangements that will continue. (b) Costs that will appear after a deal: rent (book: INR 50,000 × 3 centres × 12 = INR 18 lakh, p.6), market salary for a replacement CEO (p.46). (c) Costs that will stop after a deal: owner fees, personal expenses (pp.38, 84) |
| Financial effects | Two directions: missing costs reduce maintainable EBITDA (earnings layer 4 or 5); owner-only costs are add-backs (layer 3) |
| Possible outcomes | Margin lower than reported once arm's-length costs apply; or higher after owner costs stop |
| Evidence — public | Related-party transactions note (rent, remuneration, fees); premises ownership; key managerial remuneration |
| Evidence — diligence | Lease agreements and market rent benchmarks; expense ledgers with narration scans (p.38) |
| Credit reading | Underwrite on arm's-length costs; restrict related-party payments by covenant |
| Equity reading | "Free labour from a founder is a red flag, not a feature" (p.46): deduct market cost |
| Source | [Book] pp.6, 38, 42, 46, 77, 84 |

## CM11 — Marketing cut or spike

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Marketing as % of revenue falls before a transaction, or jumps with a one-off campaign or sponsorship |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Efficiency gains with stable acquisition cost. (b) Spend cut to flatter EBITDA before a sale (p.75). (c) One-off sponsorship or launch (book: INR 5 m sponsorship in a 15 m budget, p.43). (d) Spend now required just to hold revenue (RQ11) |
| Financial effects | A pre-deal cut needs a run-rate restoration (earnings layer 4); a one-off may be a normalisation, but only if its benefit is not also counted in revenue (Phase 2 B3) |
| Possible outcomes | Revenue weakens once spend is restored or after the cut bites |
| Evidence — public | Marketing % of revenue over three years; customer acquisition cost and customer counts where disclosed (p.43) |
| Evidence — diligence | Marketing budget vs actual; campaign-level spend and results |
| Credit reading | Treat the historical run-rate of spend as committed cost |
| Equity reading | Normalise to the spend needed to sustain revenue, not the lowest year |
| Source | [Book] pp.6, 42–43, 75 |

## CM12 — Carve-out allocations

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | A business being separated from a group (carve-out, demerger, asset sale) with shared technology, marketing, HR or corporate services |
| Competing hypotheses | (a) Allocations reflect true standalone cost. (b) Standalone cost exceeds the parent's allocation (book: Paytm ticketing moving to Zomato, now Eternal, p.16). (c) One-time separation costs (migration, rebranding) |
| Financial effects | Historical EBITDA overstates standalone earnings; a pro-forma standalone adjustment is needed (earnings layer 5); transferring employee liabilities are debt-like (V3) |
| Possible outcomes | Lower margin after separation; one-time cash costs |
| Evidence — public | Deal announcements with carve-out metrics; transition service agreements; segment disclosures of the parent |
| Evidence — diligence | Allocation keys; standalone cost build-up; employee transfer liabilities (gratuity, leave, retention) |
| Credit reading | Lend on standalone EBITDA, not the allocated history |
| Equity reading | Price separation costs and the higher run-rate cost |
| Source | [Book] pp.16, 76 |
