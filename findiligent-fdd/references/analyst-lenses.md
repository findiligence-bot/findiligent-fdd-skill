# FinDiligent Analyst Lenses

Version 1.0 · 21 Nov 2026 · Author: CA Ayush Bansal

General lessons on how to think about a company's numbers, as opposed to rules for computing them (definition files) or signals to match (pattern files). They apply to every analysis, whatever the sector. Before giving a conclusion, run each lens over it and say which ones changed the reading.

Each lens has three parts:
- **Lens:** the lesson itself.
- **How to apply:** the tests that turn the lesson into work.
- **Links:** where to look in the pattern and definition files.

Lenses come from insights the author shared through the insights inbox (D45). The lesson is labelled **[Author]**. The "How to apply" tests that Claude wrote stay **[Claude, draft]** until the author approves them (D32). Like everything else here, they are indicative, not foolproof (D36).

## AL01 — Cash from contraction is not cash earned

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Lens | Cash that comes in only because something else is shrinking can go out again just as fast. A business that is getting smaller can show strong operating cash flow for a while, because it is releasing working capital and spending less. That cash is not evidence of earning power. [Author] |
| How to apply | [Claude, draft] (1) When operating cash flow or cash conversion (R2) improves, split the gain into cash from EBITDA and cash from working-capital release. The second comes from receivables, inventory or unbilled revenue falling, often because sales fell. (2) Check whether lower capex is behind free cash flow; if capex is below depreciation, read CC07 (deferred maintenance). (3) Treat asset sales, sale-and-leaseback and lower spend on marketing, R&D or maintenance as one-off or reversible cash, not run-rate cash. (4) Ask what happens to cash when the business returns to growth. Released working capital and deferred spend build up again, so run-rate free cash flow is lower than the contraction year shows. (5) Do not grade "strong cash generation" as Supported while revenue or volume is falling, unless EBITDA-driven cash alone supports it. |
| Credit reading | Do not size debt or repayment on a contraction year's cash flow. Test DSCR (V6) on cash excluding working-capital release |
| Equity reading | Do not capitalise contraction-year free cash flow. Normalise working capital and capex to the level a growing business would need |
| Links | CC01 (receivables), CC03 (inventory), CC07 (deferred maintenance), CM11 (marketing cuts), R2 (cash conversion), R3 (capex split), W3 (normal WC level), W4 (NWC peg) |

## AL02 — Volatility versus a change in the business

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Lens | When a share price, rating or headline moves, the question is whether the business's cash flows and balance sheet have changed. If they have not, the volatility tends to pass. If they have, act, however large or well known the company is. [Author] |
| How to apply | [Claude, draft] (1) Before reacting to a price move, news story or one weak quarter, compare the cash-flow and balance-sheet facts before and after. The cash-flow facts are OCF, cash conversion (R2) and free cash flow. The balance-sheet facts are net debt (V1), leverage and DSCR (V6), liquidity, debt-like items (V3) and new obligations (BS01–BS12). (2) If none has moved beyond normal ranges, record the event as noise, with the evidence. (3) If one has moved, treat it as a finding even when the company is large, well rated or widely held. Size and reputation are not mitigants. (4) Grade the change on reported figures only. Management assurances that the change is temporary grade at most Plausible (D37). (5) Keep one-period accounting effects apart from economic change, and check against Ind AS first (D41). |
| Credit reading | A deteriorating balance sheet or cash flow triggers review, whatever the issuer's size or group backing. A price fall with unchanged fundamentals does not |
| Equity reading | Separate sentiment from fundamentals. A fall with unchanged cash flows may be an opportunity; a change in cash flows or balance sheet needs a revised thesis, whatever the market cap |
| Links | R2, V1, V3, V6, BS01–BS12, D37, D41 |
| Source | Author insight, 19 Nov 2026 (source to be confirmed) |
